Intensifying Adverbs and Adjectives

Adverb + adjective

Gradable adjectives can be made stronger with adverbs such as very, extremely and incredibly.
It’s very cold.
I was extremely tired.
Ungradable adjectives already express an extreme idea. We do not normally use them with very, extremely or incredibly. Instead, use absolutely, completely, totally or utterly.
It was absolutely brilliant.
I’m completely exhausted.
Really usually works with both gradable and ungradable adjectives.
It was really good. · It was really amazing.
Some adjectives work with intensifiers used for both groups. Notice and learn these combinations as complete phrases.
He’s very happy. · I’m totally happy.

Useful collocations

Some intensifying adverb + adjective combinations are fixed or especially common. Learn them as complete phrases.

bitterly disappointedblissfully happybrutally honestfiercely competitivesorely missed
She’s always been fiercely competitive.
To be brutally honest, this is not good enough.
You’ll be sorely missed when you leave.

Adjective + noun

The adjective forms absolute, complete, total and utter can intensify nouns.
absolute + nounIt’s an absolute mess.
complete + nounI’m in complete shock.
total + nounIt’s total chaos.
utter + nounIt’s an utter disaster.

Combining intensifiers

We sometimes join absolute(ly), complete(ly), total(ly) and utter(ly) with and for extra emphasis. Utter(ly) commonly comes second.
It was totally and utterly unfair.
It’s a complete and utter disgrace.
We do not usually combine very, really, extremely and incredibly with and. However, we can repeat an intensifier.
She’s very, very lucky. · It was really, really interesting.

Informal intensifier: super

In informal English, super can intensify some adjectives.
The museum is super interesting.
The place is super cool.
Developing conversations

Reinforcing, emphasizing and exemplifying a point

We often use a simile to emphasize or exaggerate a point in an anecdote. The listener may respond with Really?, Yeah? or a similar reaction. The speaker then reinforces the point with another example introduced by Honestly, Absolutely, I swear, and similar expressions.

Simile or strong image→Listener’s reaction→Reinforcement + example
A · STRONG IMAGEIt’s like hitting this thick wall of heat.
B · REACTIONGosh. Really?
A · REINFORCEMENTHonestly. You’d just die if you walked for any length of time.
