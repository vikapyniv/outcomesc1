Phrasal verbs

How phrasal verbs work, whether they take an object, and where that object belongs.

verb+particle

A phrasal verb consists of a verb and a particle such as on, up, down or in. Sometimes the meaning is clear from the combination, and sometimes it is very different from the separate words.

Transparent meaningagree on · focus on · believe in
Idiomatic meaningfall out · give in · take up

1. Intransitive phrasal verbs

They do not take an object.

I grew up in Scotland.
The relationship broke down.
We have always got on well.

2. Transitive phrasal verbs

They must take an object. When the object is a verb, use the -ing form.

I came across an old school photo.
We’ve decided to put off releasing the information.

3. Object after the particle

With some verbs, the object always follows the particle.

I ran into an old friend.
✓ We agree on most things.
I ran an old friend into yesterday.

4. Two particles

The object always goes after the second particle.

I need to read up on it.
Things usually boil down to money.

5. Separable phrasal verbs

The object may go before or after the particle. However, a pronoun always goes before the particle.

Noun: both positions are possible.
They called the wedding off. / They called off the wedding.
Pronoun: it must separate the verb and particle.
✓ They called it off.
They called off it.

6. Two objects

Some phrasal verbs can be followed by two objects.

My family helped get me through some tough times.
Jim got me out of a difficult situation.

7. Passive voice

Some transitive phrasal verbs can be used in the passive. Keep the particle with the verb.

The wedding has been called off.
The company was set up in 2020.
Learning tip: there is no simple rule that identifies each type. When you meet a new phrasal verb, notice whether it takes an object and where that object appears.
